Assume a uniform field with orientation specified by polar coordinates.
phi is the angle between the field vector and the z axis.
theta is the angle between the x axis 
and the projection of the field vector onto the x axis.

Assume a isotropic conductive medium.
The isopotential surfaces are perpendicular to the orientation of the field, 
and uniformly spaced.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that the potential is 0 
for all points on the plane that passes through (0,0,0).
Let this be called the zero potential plane.
The potential at any point xyz is proportional to the distance of that point
from the zero potential plane.
That distance is given by the dot product of the field's unit vector (i.e. 
the unit vector that is orthogonal to the zero potential plane, and 
oriented in the same direction as the field)
i.e.
  ufield = ux*sin(phi)*cos(theta) + uy*sin(phi)*sin(theta) + uz*cos(phi)
    where ux, uy, and uz are the unit vectors along the x, y, and z axes
and 
the vector that has its origin at (0, 0, 0) and terminates at (x , y, z)
i.e.
  p = ux*x + uy*y + uz*z

That is,
  V(x,y,z) = -k * p dot ufield
           = -k * (x*sin(phi)*cos(theta) + y*sin(phi)*sin(theta) + z*cos(phi))
           = -k * d
where
  d is the distance of the point from the zero potential plane
  k is the field intensity in units of potential/distance, and is >= 0.

Note:  why the minus sign in front of k?
Because a positive charge _loses_ potential energy as it moves in the 
direction of the field ("lines of electrical field move away from 
the positive electrode and toward the negative electrode").

So the parameters are
  Orientation
    phi    angle from the z axis
    theta  angle of projection onto the xy plane, measured CCW from the x axis
  Strength
    k      field intensity in (millivolt/micron)

For each internal node calculate d.
Let the stimulus waveform specify the field intensity.
